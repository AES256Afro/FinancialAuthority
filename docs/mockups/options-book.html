3 · Paychecks

Six ways to see what a check does

All six draw from paycheck.plan and cashflow.forecast, the same rows the by-check table shows now. Each becomes a registered picture; any section that shows a paycheck gets a "show as" menu, and the home views take the one you chose. Colours stay in the validated palette: bills orange, debt purple, kept blue, yours green, set-aside grey.

The ribbon

One check, split into where it goes

The one you remembered. Width is dollars. Hover a band for its bills; a second level opens "bills" into each biller. Two checks side by side when a window spans both.

Strips over time

Ten checks, the trend of what is left

Past checks solid, projected ones faded. The solid line is the net; the dashed line is what the check had to cover. The gap between them is yours, and the trend of that gap is the whole story.

The waterfall

Net down to safe, step by step

Reads left to right like a receipt. Good for "why is safe to spend that number": every step is a row you can open.

The stream

Six months, the bands widen and narrow

When a pay-later plan ends, its band thins and the green widens. The shape shows the direction without a single number.

Rings

One ring per check, yours in the middle

Small multiples. Four checks in a row make the mid and end rhythm obvious: the mid check carries the card, the end check carries rent.

Beads on a thread

Between two paydays, when each bill lands

Size is dollars, position is the day. The bead you can slide is a due date you can change; the engine's date-move check runs as you drag.

Agency: these are not eight new home views. They are six pictures of one result, and any place a paycheck appears (home, Bills, Move, Borrowing, the exports) offers the menu. A view is then a layout of sections, and each section remembers its picture.
4 · Saving

The ladder, the set-aside, the rollover

A section for someone who has never saved or who watched others never save. Rungs instead of a vague "emergency fund"; a set-aside the engine suggests from what is actually left; and the moment a debt ends, its payment keeps going, into saving, unless you say otherwise.

The rollover

When a plan ends, the payment keeps going

The $410 a check that went to pay-later ends in February. The engine proposes it rolls into the ladder from March, and shows rung 2 landing in May instead of October. One tap keeps it, one tap frees it.

5 · Guide

Hold the hand without holding the wallet

First run, five screens

For someone who has never looked
  1. When are you paid? (the cadence, two taps)
  2. Connect a bank or drop a file (either is fine)
  3. Name the bills it found (one list, swipe to keep or drop)
  4. Which check pays which bill (a drag per bill)
  5. Your first picture (the view picker, with your own numbers in each thumbnail)

Nothing is asked twice; every screen can be skipped and reopened from Next steps.

The one thing today

Plus streaks that mean something
Today · Wed Oct 15● On track

Pay the Blue card $980 before Oct 22. After that, nothing until payday.

31 days without an advance6 bills on time in a rowrung 1 in 29 days

The brief already lists everything; this picks the single action with the biggest effect and says why. Streaks are computed from the ledger, so they cannot be gamed by tapping.

"Why this number"

On every figure, in plain words
Safe to spend $810
Your Oct 15 check was $3,120. Before the next payday, $980 goes to the card, $410 to pay-later, $420 to bills, $300 is set aside for rent, $200 to savings. What is left is $810. Rows: 6 · Not financial advice.

The sources already exist on every result. This is a plain-language sentence generated by the engine, not a model, so it is always true.

The weekly check-in

Five taps on a Sunday
  • Anything unexpected this week? (the three largest new charges, keep or flag)
  • Still paying for these? (one subscription a week)
  • The card: paid before the statement? (yes, or a reminder)
  • Savings moved? (yes, or the sheet)
  • Next week's one thing

Ends with a single sentence of where you stand against last week. Sent as a push if you want it, never as an email without approval.

9 · Hardening

Security and bug sweep, one milestone, written down

Security

Verify from outside, not from the code
  • Session cookie: Secure, HttpOnly, SameSite strict; the CSRF header on every write; a sign-in rate limit and lockout, tested from another machine
  • Security headers: a content-security policy, frame denial, referrer and permissions policies; checked by a test that fetches the live page
  • OAuth: token lifetimes, refresh rotation, PKCE required, revoke-all; a client at every scope tried against every tool (M9.11)
  • Exports: session only, no-store, the redaction level applied, one audit row each; a test that an export never contains a secret
  • Uploads and parsers: size limits, malformed PDF and CSV fuzzing, a timeout per document
  • Secrets in logs: a job run end to end, then the log grepped for every FA_ value, in CI
  • Dependency audit in CI (pip-audit, npm audit); the image rebuilt on a base-image fix
  • Backups: encrypted, restored to a clean container and compared (M9.8); the key rotation drill (M9.9)

Bugs and readability

Every screen, three widths, three states
  • Every page at phone, tablet and desktop width with empty, loading and error states, screenshotted from the fixture and kept as golden images
  • Every view against the fixture in light and dark, the palette validator run on each
  • Number formatting: one function, cents only where cents matter, thousands separators everywhere
  • Dates at the edges: month-end on a weekend, the 31st, daylight-saving days, the year boundary
  • Keyboard reach on every control, focus visible, reduced motion honoured
  • A glossary of the twenty words the app uses, linked from the first use on each page
  • Copy pass: every sentence says what the thing does; no jargon that a first-time saver would not know
10 · Reach

Reminders where you are; the phone, the tablet, the wrist

Installable, with push

Free, this month
  • Add to Home Screen on iPhone and iPad: icon, full screen, the Today page as the start
  • Web push from the app itself (iOS 16.4 and later), no ntfy needed; ntfy and Gotify stay as options
  • The Apple Watch shows every iPhone notification: the brief, the one thing, "pay the card by Oct 22"
  • Reminders before due dates, on payday, and when a statement day is near, each a switch
  • Email reminders only through the approval gate, as the rules require
  • Siri Shortcuts: "what is safe to spend" over the REST with a read key; a widget through the Shortcuts app
  • The ICS feed: paydays and due dates in the phone's own calendar

This is M7.5 finished plus push. Everything in it is free and runs on BigBox.

Native iPhone, iPad, Watch

Feasible; one cost to decide
  • One SwiftUI app for iPhone and iPad; a watch app with a complication: safe to spend, days to payday, the one thing
  • Home-screen widgets: safe to spend, cover, the next bill; Lock Screen widgets on the phone
  • Reads the same REST through an OAuth client with the read scope; approvals stay in the web app, so the phone app can never send
  • Nothing stored on the device but the token; Face ID to open

The cost. Running it on your own devices from Xcode is free but needs a Mac and a re-sign every seven days. TestFlight or the App Store needs the Apple Developer Program, $99 a year, which is the only paid thing in this whole book. The web app above gets most of the way without it. Android later from the same REST.
