# FinancialAuthority roadmap

*Written 2026-10-14, revised the same day. The thinking behind it is in [PLAN.md](PLAN.md); what the owner grants is in [ACCESS.md](ACCESS.md). Marks: ☐ planned, 🔄 in progress, ✅ done (version). Effort is for one developer working with coding agents. Each milestone is also a GitHub milestone on the repository.*

Order matters more than dates. Real transactions are live by the end of M1. Claude can reach the data from the phone by the end of M3. Both mailboxes are in by the end of M4. The paycheck plan exists at the end of M5. The app runs itself at the end of M6. All eight views and three looks are in by the end of M7. v1.0 ships at the end of M8, about eighteen weeks in. The feature inventory from the leading apps is in [FEATURES.md](FEATURES.md); each row there names its milestone below.

## M2 — Obligations, payments, paystubs (2 weeks)

- 🔄 **M2.1 Bill registry** (2026-10-14). Each recurring series is confirmed as a bill, a subscription, income, an installment plan, or ignored; manual add; the Bills page edits amount, due day, autopay and whether the date can move. *Left: the paying account, grace days, late fees and the allowed move window in the UI.* *2026-10-15: each detected series carries a suggested kind from its category (bill, subscription, or regular spending to ignore), shown as the dark button on Needs you; the owner's tap still decides.* *Due days run to the 31st (the last day the month has), and each bill says which paycheck pays it: the one before its due date, the 15th check or the end-of-month check, so rent due on the 1st rides the end-of-month check.*
- 🔄 **M2.2 Debts** (2026-10-14). The Debts page: the mountain with each debt's share, the minimums due a month, the interest the month costs, and the two classic payoff orders; installment plans and advances counted as debt; `debts_list`. *Left: the edit form (balance, APR, minimum, statement day, due day), balances kept current from the connectors.* *2026-10-15: every card account carries a debt row; the line arrives from the bank, the APR, statement day, due day and paid-in-full are typed on the Debts page; utilisation and the statement date drive the credit picture on Borrowing. Planned payments: money the owner says is leaving before the ledger sees it is counted from its date, netted off the card it goes to, and settles itself when the transaction posts. Spending accounts hold their available balance.*
- 🔄 **M2.3 Installment plans** (2026-10-14). Biweekly series are detected and linked to known plans or confirmed as plans from Needs you. *Left: the thirty-second form with provider, total and schedule.* *2026-10-15: Affirm's loan-confirmation email is read deterministically into a plan with the lender's own schedule (every payment's date, principal and interest), the loan amount, the finance charge and the loan ID, which keeps one email from making two plans; the ledger ticks payments off; the Borrowing page lists the loans with what each costs. Affirm has no API or export; the email is the sync.* *Affirm's monthly Loans Statement PDF, the one export Affirm offers, is read deterministically: every loan by ID with its balance, APR, next payment and status, the interest per loan and for the year; uploaded on Borrowing or dropped in the import folder; the Debts page carries the lender's balances. A plan knows the account that pays it; one charged to a card rides that card.*
- 🔄 **M2.4 Advances.** Earned-wage and cash-advance apps: amount, fee, repayment date, repaid or not; detected from the pair of transactions and confirmed. *2026-10-15: advances are read from the ledger alone: money in from an advance app paired with the repayment that covers it, or a run of partials, and the fee between them; one still out is due on the next payday and held back. Every borrowing row is filed under its own Borrowing category by rule and never counted as spending. The Borrowing page draws the cost, the cycle by payday, each app's effective rate, the installment streams with their end dates, what the fees would become, and the steps out; the brief, the watchlist and the next steps carry it; the card balance is held back from the coming checks as the owner pays it.*
- 🔄 **M2.5 Payment matching** (2026-10-14). Each bill period finds its payment by name and amount, or by exact amount alone; unmatched periods go late, then past due; `payments_list`; the bill board shows it. *Left: the Payments page and the "pay this now" list.*
- 🔄 **M2.6 Paystubs** (2026-10-14). A PDF attachment on any message that reads like a paystub becomes a pending fact: gross, net, pay date, period, hours and every deduction line; confirming records the stub, matches the deposit, compares the net with the usual and keeps the paycheck's expected net current; the Paystubs page draws the gross-to-net bar and the six-paycheck strip; `paystubs_list`. *Left: the import folder's paystubs subfolder, the owner's payroll format checked against a real stub, year-to-date columns, OCR for scanned stubs.* *2026-10-15: the columns earnings-statement layout (Pay Date, Period Start and End, Earnings, W/H Taxes, Deductions, Net Pay with year-to-date) reads deterministically with rate, hours, pre-tax flags and the deposit's last four; a stub PDF can be uploaded on the Paystubs page or dropped in the import folder, and waits on Needs you.*
- ☐ **M2.7 Subscriptions.** Price history, last charge, category, duplicate detection.
- 🔄 **M2.8 Calendar and snapshot** (2026-10-14). Paydays, due dates, installments and advance repayments on one grid, sixty days ahead, with each week's low point; `finance_snapshot` carries the windows, the calendar, the payments and the brief. *Left: `debts_list`, `installments_list`, `advances_list`, `subscriptions_list` as their own tools.* *2026-10-15: safe to spend is until payday, from checking only (savings is kept and shown apart), less what is due before payday, advances pulled before it, the buffer and any shortfall the next paycheck leaves in its own window; the parts are returned with it.* *Each bill knows the account it is charged to, learned from its own charges and changeable on the Bills page; a bill on the card rides the card payment it is paid from instead of leaving checking on its due date, and the card payment carries the balance, the bills that rode it and the usual card spend until it is paid. The pace is split by what pays it. The Rocket Money export merges with the bank link's months and lands its years on the same accounts.*
- 🔄 **M2.9 Accounts and net worth** (2026-10-14; the Accounts page: every balance with its connector and freshness, net worth as what you have less what you owe, stale balances named. *Left: the daily history, investment balances.*) Every account with its balance, connector and freshness; net worth as assets minus debts, kept daily; investment balances read-only where a connector offers them; `networth.history`.

Done when: every recurring charge in the fixture and in the real data is classified, last month's bills each show their payment, the owner's last three paystubs are parsed and matched to their deposits, and the calendar shows the next sixty days correctly.

## M5 — The guidance engine (2.5 weeks)

- ☐ **M5.1 Cash-flow projection.** Daily balance per account for ninety days from confirmed bills, series, paychecks, advances, minimums and installments, with a what-if layer.
- ☐ **M5.2 Paycheck plan.** Advances first, then obligations in the window, set-asides, minimums, buffer, goals, safe-to-spend, with every line's source; the "pay this now" list beside it.
- ✅ **M5.3 Purchase check** (2026-10-14). Yes, wait or no over the sixty-day honest line: a purchase passes when it leaves the line where it is above zero and no deeper where it is already under; otherwise the first thing it breaks with the shortfall, and the first date it would pass; the pay-in-four variant; `purchase_check` and the card on Today. Property tests: nothing costs nothing; the first passing date passes.
- 🔄 **M5.4 The don't-buy list and watchlists** (2026-10-14: the list as an engine function with sources, a tool, a route and the "Hold off on" card on Today: active advances, too many plans, the buffer dip, price rises, duplicate subscriptions, a pay-in-full card carrying a balance, categories over their usual. *Left: owner-set category targets and watch words.*) Buffer breaches, categories over target, duplicate subscriptions, price increases, too many plans or any active advance, a pay-in-full card carrying a balance. Watchlists: a soft cap on any category, merchant or tag with a month-end projection; they warn and project, never block.
- 🔄 **M5.5 Billing-date optimiser** (2026-10-14). Every due day tried for every movable bill; the moves that lift the sixty-day low point by a dollar or more, ranked, each judged alone, with the before and after, the instruction for the biller and a draft request; `billing_dates_suggest` and the card on Bills. Property test: a move never lowers the low point, and the predicted low point is the one the move yields. *Left: combined moves, the move window per biller, drafts queued for approval once sending exists (M4.5).*
- 🔄 **M5.6 Rules and goals** (2026-10-14: standing rules and goals as text both models read; the buffer; save-up and pay-down goals with a target, a date and a pace, the engine's needed-per-paycheck, on-track or not, what they take from safe-to-spend beside the headline, the Goals card on Today with record-and-remove. *Left: pay-in-full and never-touch flags on the registers, category targets, goal priorities in the plan's order, `notes.remember`.*) Original scope: Buffer, pay-in-full, never-touch accounts, category targets, goal priorities; `notes.remember`. Save-up goals (target, date, pace, funded from the aqua band, with on-track, ahead and at-risk states) and pay-down goals (which debt, how much extra, the payoff date it buys), both visible in the paycheck plan and the debt mountain.
- ✅ **M5.9 The outlook** (2026-10-14, at the owner's ask). Savings and investments carried forward from today's balances and the contributions on record (the paystubs' retirement lines, the goals' pace) at rates the owner sets as assumptions: three scenarios by year, nominal and in today's dollars, the savings balance by month, a retirement line with the monthly draw at the owner's withdrawal rate; and "where does X a paycheck do the most": the same take-home dollars sent to a savings account, the 401(k) with its match and tax treatment, a Roth IRA, a brokerage account, an HSA, or paying down each debt at its APR, ranked by what is kept after tax, with whether each is guaranteed and reachable and the step that starts it. The Outlook page, `outlook` and `where_to_put`. Tests: compounding matches the textbook, scenarios keep their order, today's dollars sit below nominal, the ranking carries sources and steps.
- ☐ **M5.7 Evaluation.** Golden outputs on the fixture; property tests (never below the buffer, totals reconcile, the optimiser never worsens the minimum balance); the citation test.
- ☐ **M5.8 Tools.** `cashflow.forecast`, `paycheck.plan`, `purchase.check`, `billing_dates.suggest`, `notes.remember`.

Done when: on the fixture the plan matches its golden output, the optimiser finds the known better schedule, and a local 8 B model explains a plan without a number that is not in the engine's result.

## M17 — Switching banks (proposed and built 2026-10-16)

The owner is moving the banking to a credit union over a few months, the card later. The app plans the move from its own registers and ledger, with nothing outside it.

- ✅ **M17.1 Find everything.** The paycheck, every bill the old account pays, the cards it pays, the pay-later plans on it, the advance apps that draw on it (from the ledger and the advances register), and the recurring charges and transfers the ledger sees there that no register names. A bill that only defaulted to the old account is made explicit first, so moving the paycheck never silently moves the bills.
- ✅ **M17.2 A window for each.** The paycheck first, with payroll's lead time. Then each bill after its next charge on the old account and after the money is in the new one, with five days before its first charge on the new; a charge too close stays put one more cycle. Subscriptions wait for the new debit card. Advance apps are suggested to stay behind and end.
- ✅ **M17.3 The money through the move.** Each pay window, what lands in and leaves each account with the switches as planned, and what to move between them so neither runs short. Test: the total in and out is the same as with no move.
- ✅ **M17.4 The ledger confirms.** A charge or a deposit on the new account confirms a switch; one still landing on the old account after a switch is an alert. A job reads the ledger every six hours and finds anything new on the old account. Alerts reach the one thing today and the reminders.
- ✅ **M17.5 Close out.** The old account can close once everything that moved is confirmed and nothing has landed on it for 45 days.
- ☐ **M17.6 The card.** When the credit union's card arrives, a second move from the old card to the new one: the same page, the card as the source.
- ✅ **M17.6a What the first real move taught.** An advance left behind still repays from the old account, so its repayment stays in the money table. Card payments switch around the days the plan pays them, not the due date. A wallet (PayPal and the like) is one item, and ATM cash is not a transfer to switch. When the old account is charged within three days of the first paycheck landing in the new one, too soon for a transfer, the plan asks for a payroll split and says how much.
- ☐ **M17.7 Real move, by the owner.** The credit union added to the app (linked through Plaid, or by hand); the direct deposit form to payroll; each switch tapped as it is made. The move itself is started; specifics are in `docs/INVENTORY.local.md`.

**Done when:** the old checking account closes with every item confirmed on the new one, no payment late, and no window short.
